Six editions, one identity: designing Greener Summit year after year

How a 2022 rebrand became the system behind Brazil's C-level energy meeting, and how my role around it grew from art direction to the event's operations.

Laura Magrin

Greener Summit brings together the decision-makers of Brazil's energy transition for two days of strategic content, networking and deal-making. I have led its marketing and art direction in every edition since 2022. Looking back across six editions, the most useful thing I built for the event was not a single campaign but an identity that could carry all of them.

Starting from a rebrand

When I joined the project in 2022, the event was still called Greener Business Summit. Greener's brand as a whole had just evolved, and the event had to follow: the logo, the colours and the concept all changed. That identity is still the event's visual reference today.

An event identity has to work as a system. The same language has to hold on the stage, on the signage, on the LED screens and in every campaign piece, and it has to survive a new theme, new speakers and new sponsors every year.

From art direction to the whole experience

In 2024 I was one of the heads of the project. Beyond art direction and the event identity, that meant print production, the wayfinding rules on site, institutional communication and the marketing strategy for promotion and stakeholder engagement, while sitting on the execution committee.

In 2025 I led communication planning and positioning, and the design team's execution, from backdrops and signage to motion content for the LED panels.

2026: adding the operational layer

In 2026 the event took the theme “The New Energy Economy” and brought together 200+ participants from 130+ companies, with 40+ speakers and 12+ hours of content. This time I also took on the event's operational layer: the technical runsheet for the audio, video and screen crew, the photography coverage plan and the brand exposure reports for sponsors.

The campaign ran 80+ email sends with 240K+ views and a 31% open rate, plus real-time coverage with facial-recognition photo delivery to attendees. On Instagram, 37 Summit posts reached 26K+ views at a 5% engagement rate.

What carries over from one edition to the next

  • Design the identity as a system, not as a poster. The theme changes every year; the system is what keeps the event recognisable.
  • Bring design into operations. Runsheets, wayfinding and coverage plans decide how the identity is actually experienced on the day.
  • Report back to sponsors. Brand exposure reports close the loop with the companies that make the event possible.

A team effort

None of this is a one-person job. Most editions were made side by side with Marco Conte, the event's Project Owner from 2023 to 2025, Silvana Silvestre on sales and relationships and Mateus Pinheiro on content, together with the design team: Renan Corazza, Arthur Laprano and Viviane Custódio.
